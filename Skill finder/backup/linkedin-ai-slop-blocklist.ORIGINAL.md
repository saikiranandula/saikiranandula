# AI-Slop Blocklist & Self-Check (LinkedIn)

The failure mode that kills a thought-leadership profile is sounding like a generated LinkedIn post. A senior product leader or recruiter spots it in two lines and discounts everything after. It also costs reach: LinkedIn punishes templated content indirectly through low dwell, no saves, and no real discussion. So this file is a *distribution tool*, not just taste.

This is the enforceable checklist, distilled from **AISLOPOPEDIA** (the encyclopedia of AI tells, written by the model that produces them) and extended with LinkedIn-native patterns. **Run every draft against it before publishing — posts, carousels, and comments.**

**How to use:** these are *high-suspicion patterns*, not a banned-words list. One may be fine; a cluster means rewrite. The test: if a phrase is doing rhetorical work *instead of* carrying a fact, a number, a mechanism, or a real decision, cut it. The source's own thesis: *"These patterns aren't bad because they're wrong. They're bad because they're overused and invisible. When everything sounds the same, nothing sounds like anything."* Sai's edge is sounding like one specific product leader with specific evidence, not the median LinkedIn post.

## Openers (most damaging — they set the read)

- **Throat-clearers** (delay the point by a sentence): "Here's the thing:", "Here's what most people miss:", "Here's what nobody's talking about:", "Here's the uncomfortable truth:", "Let me be clear:", "I'll be honest:", "Can we talk about X for a second?", "Let's talk about X.", "We need to talk about X." → Just say the point. Open with the observed number, contradiction, or product consequence.
- **False exclusivity:** "Nobody's talking about this.", "Most people won't tell you this.", "Everyone's sleeping on this.", "This flew under the radar.", "What they don't want you to know:", "The secret X doesn't want you to know:", "Most people get this completely wrong.", "99% of PMs do this backwards." → Real insight stands without the conspiracy framing.
- **Manufactured urgency:** "Stop what you're doing.", "Drop everything.", "Read this before X.", "You're going to want to bookmark this.", "This changes everything.", "This is bigger than people realize.", "X just changed the game forever.", "PSA:".
- **Fake vulnerability / manufactured story:** "I'm going to be honest with you.", "I wasn't going to post this, but…", "This is scary to share.", "Hot take incoming (don't hate me):", "Unpopular opinion:", "I might lose followers for this, but…", and the rejection/failure cold-open ("Last week I got rejected.", "I got fired in 2019.", "I cried in my car after that meeting."). The too-neat anecdote that resolves into a tidy lesson ("A PM on my team asked me one question. It changed how I lead.") is the single most inauthentic-reading pattern to a senior audience. Avoid the whole genre. If a real story is genuinely the best vehicle, tell it plainly with specifics, no engineered arc and no "here's what it taught me:" hinge.

## Body patterns

- **Binary contrast — the single most overused AI structure.** "It's not about X. It's about Y.", "The problem isn't X. It's Y.", "X isn't the future. Y is.", "Most people think it's X. It's actually Y.", "The bottleneck isn't X. It's Y.", "X is dead. Y is what's next.", "Forget X. Focus on Y.", "The question isn't X. The question is Y." → Use a contrast only when specific evidence carries it, and rarely. Banned otherwise.
- **Fill-in-the-blank thought-leader templates:** "X isn't just Y. It's Z." ("AI isn't just a tool. It's a thought partner."), "The best PMs don't write specs. They design systems.", "In 2026, AI literacy won't be optional. It'll be table stakes.", "X is the new Y." ("Prompt engineering is the new programming."), "If you're still doing X, you're already behind.", "The PM of 2026 will look nothing like the PM of 2024.", "Your agent is only as good as your context window." → These are LinkedIn-generator output. Swap-the-noun templates. Rewrite around the actual decision or number.
- **False agency** (giving things human powers to dodge naming who acted): "The data speaks for itself.", "The market has spoken.", "The numbers don't lie.", "AI is coming for your job.", "The industry is waking up to…", "The implications are staggering.", "The possibilities are endless." → Name the actor and the number.
- **Numbered-list-as-synthesis / artificial scarcity:** "5 things I learned from X:", "3 mistakes everyone makes:", "I spent 100+ hours so you don't have to.", "I read 50 papers on X. Here's the summary:", "I analyzed 1,000 X. Here's what I found:" → The real insight count in a "10 things" list is usually 2. Use structure only when content is genuinely list-shaped; the main post body should be paragraphs.
- **"I asked ChatGPT" / time-lapse brag:** "I asked ChatGPT to X and the results shocked me.", "I built this in 2 hours.", "From zero to X in 48 hours.", "What used to take 3 months now takes 3 minutes." → Lazy AI-era formats; for a product leader they read as unserious.
- **Pivot phrases** (fake connective tissue): "But here's where it gets interesting:", "And here's the kicker:", "But that's not even the best part.", "Wait, it gets better.", "Now here's the thing:", "But the real story is:", "Plot twist:", "Enter: X." → Use real logical connection or a hard fact to move forward.
- **Qualifier sandwich** (hedging around every claim): "It's worth noting that…", "To be fair…", "That said…", "Don't get me wrong…", "Granted, [obvious counterpoint]…" → Say the claim or drop it.
- **Dramatic fragmentation** (choppy lines as fake style): "Let that sink in.", "Read that again.", "Full stop.", "Period.", "That's it. That's the post.", "This. Is. The. Future.", "Sit with that for a second.", "I'll say it louder for the people in the back." → A real paragraph beats fragments-as-style.

## Closers ("LinkedIn's final boss" per the source)

- **CTA slop:** "What do you think? Drop your take below.", "Agree or disagree?", "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "What would you add?", "Follow for more X content.", "Repost if this resonated.", "Save this for later.", "Tag someone who needs to hear this.", "Link in comments." → Close on an earned, specific thesis. No CTA.
- **Engagement-bait gating (hard block):** "Comment 'GUIDE' and I'll send you the template.", "Want the checklist? Comment 'YES'.", "DM me 'AI' for the playbook.", "Repost ♻️ to help your network.", "Follow me for daily PM posts." → This is the dominant 2026 reach-hack and the fastest way to look like a growth-hacker, not a leader. Never. If there's a deeper asset, offer it as an open link, no gating.
- **Fake philosophical closer** (profound-sounding, says nothing): "The question isn't whether, but when.", "We're still early.", "This is just the beginning.", "The genie is out of the bottle.", "Buckle up.", "Welcome to the future."
- **Performative mic drop:** "And that changes everything.", "Think about that.", "This is the new normal.", "Act accordingly.", "Plan accordingly.", "X will never be the same." → Close on an earned, specific implication instead.
- **Humble-brag-as-announcement:** "Humbled and honored to announce…", "Grateful doesn't begin to cover it.", "Still pinching myself." → Avoid the genre.

## LinkedIn formatting tells (visual, not just verbal)

- **Broetry / one-sentence-per-line spacing.** Every sentence its own paragraph with blank lines, engineered for the "…see more" cutoff. Catch: 5+ consecutive single-line paragraphs. Use real paragraphs; let the argument flow. Some line breaks for a genuinely standalone line are fine; the *wall of single lines* is the tell.
- **"…see more" cliffhanger hook** deliberately cut mid-thought ("The email said three words…"). The hook should be a complete, specific observation, not a withheld one.
- **Emoji bullets and section headers:** "✅", "🔹", "➡️" lists; "🔑 Key takeaway:", "⚡ The shift:", "📌 Bottom line:" headers; the strategic single hook emoji (🚀💡🔥🧵👀🎯). House rule: **no emojis in the post body.** A carousel may use the restrained accent system in `visual-system.md`, never emoji.
- **Orphan arrows:** "↓", "👇" on their own line pointing at a CTA or "link in comments." Cut.
- **All-caps single-word emphasis lines:** "This.", "STOP.", "Read." Cut.
- **Em-dash overuse.** House rule: no em-dashes at all. Use commas or periods.

## Word-level tells

- **Adverbs that scream AI** (3+ in a paragraph = AI wrote it): literally, incredibly, fundamentally, genuinely, essentially, significantly, arguably, undeniably, remarkably, interestingly, notably, particularly, ultimately.
- **Adjectives that mean nothing:** robust, seamless, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, revolutionary, transformative, comprehensive, holistic, game-changing, next-level, world-class.
- **Verbs that sound smart but aren't:** leverage, navigate (challenges), unpack (concepts), double down, spearhead, supercharge, unlock, streamline, reimagine, synergize.
- **Corporate-LinkedIn filler:** "move the needle," "north star," "low-hanging fruit," "circle back," "drive impact," "deliver value," "at the end of the day," "in today's fast-paced world," "let's dive in," "let's unpack this," "this is a masterclass in X," "chef's kiss," flat rule-of-three ("Clear. Concise. Compelling.").

## Comment / reply slop (high-signal comments are core to the hiring play)

- **Agreement slop:** "This.", "Exactly this.", "So well said.", "This is gold.", "Couldn't agree more.", "Spot on.", "Saving this." → A comment must add a data point, a correction, a precedent, or a product-strategy implication, or it doesn't ship.
- **Engagement-farming comment:** "Counterpoint:" + rephrase, "Hot take:" + restate the post, "As someone who [credentials], I can confirm."
- **Humble-brag response:** "As someone who's been doing this for 10 years…", "I literally just had this conversation with my CEO.", "My team was just discussing this."
- Open the comment with the insight, never with flattery.

## The high-risk tells for a product-leader voice specifically

These read as inauthentic fastest to the exact senior audience Sai is trying to reach, and are precisely what an LLM defaults to when prompted "write a thought-leadership LinkedIn post." Enforce hardest against:
1. The manufactured-mentorship anecdote ("A junior PM asked me…").
2. The fake-metric brag with a tidy framework ("We 10x'd activation in 6 weeks. Here's the 5-step framework.").
3. The "shipping is a mindset" / reframe-template post.
4. Comment-gating CTAs.

## Self-check before publishing (run every time)

1. Could the first two lines sit unchanged under any AI announcement or any generic LinkedIn post? If yes, rewrite with a concrete actor, number, constraint, or real decision.
2. Scan for any opener, template, pivot, fragmentation, or closer pattern above. Cut or replace with a fact.
3. Count vague adverbs/adjectives/verbs. If a sentence leans on them instead of a number or mechanism, rewrite it.
4. Does every paragraph carry a fact, a comparison, a mechanism, or an advance in the argument? If a line only decorates, delete it.
5. Is there at least one sentence only Sai would write because of his product lens (a real tradeoff, adoption bottleneck, or operating consequence)? If not, the post is generic. Add it or kill the post.
6. Would Sai say this out loud to another PM in a serious conversation? If it would sound like a performance, rewrite.
7. House rules: no em-dashes, no emojis in the body, no engagement-bait CTA, no broetry wall of single lines.
