# AI-Slop Blocklist & Self-Check

The failure mode that kills Nullhype's credibility (and reach — Grox can read these patterns) is sounding like generic AI-generated content. This file is the enforceable checklist, distilled from AISLOPOPEDIA (the encyclopedia of AI tells, written by the model that produced them). Run every draft against it before publishing.

**How to use:** these are not "never write these words" rules — they are *high-suspicion patterns*. One may be fine; a cluster means rewrite. The test: if a phrase is doing rhetorical work *instead of* a fact, mechanism, or number, cut it.

## Openers (the most damaging — they set the read)

- **Throat-clearers** (delay the point by one sentence): "Here's the thing:", "Here's what most people miss:", "Here's what nobody's talking about:", "Here's the uncomfortable truth:", "Let me be clear:", "I'll be honest:", "Can we talk about X for a second?", "We need to talk about X." → Just say the point.
- **False exclusivity:** "Nobody's talking about this.", "What they don't want you to know:", "The secret X doesn't want you to know:", "I wasn't supposed to share this, but…", "Flew under the radar." → If it's real insight, it stands without the conspiracy framing.
- **Manufactured urgency:** "Stop what you're doing.", "Drop everything.", "Read this before X.", "Save this before it gets taken down.", "This changes everything.", "This is bigger than people realize.", "X just changed the game forever."
- **Fake vulnerability:** "Hot take incoming (don't hate me):", "Unpopular opinion:", "I might lose followers for this, but…", "This is scary to share.", "I know I'll get hate for this, but…" (Reality: the "unpopular opinion" is always something 95% agree with.)

## Body patterns

- **Binary contrast — the single most overused AI structure.** "It's not about X. It's about Y.", "The problem isn't X. It's Y.", "X isn't the future. Y is.", "Most people think it's X. It's actually Y.", "The bottleneck isn't X. It's Y.", "X is dead. Y is what's next.", "Forget X. Focus on Y." → Nullhype already bans unearned "not X, but Y." Use it only when the contrast is carried by specific evidence, and at most rarely.
- **False agency** (giving inanimate things human powers to avoid naming who acted): "The data speaks for itself.", "The market has spoken.", "The numbers don't lie.", "This technology wants to…", "AI is coming for your X.", "The industry is waking up to…", "The implications are staggering.", "The possibilities are endless." → Name the actor and the number.
- **Pivot phrases** (fake connective tissue between paragraphs): "But here's where it gets interesting:", "And here's the kicker:", "But that's not even the best part.", "Wait, it gets better.", "Now here's the thing:", "But the real story is:", "And that's just the beginning.", "But wait, there's more." → Use real logical connection or a hard fact to move forward.
- **Dramatic fragmentation** (choppy sentences as fake style): "Let that sink in.", "Read that again.", "Full stop.", "Period.", "That's it. That's the tweet.", "This. Is. The. Future.", "Sit with that for a second.", "I'll say it louder for the people in the back." → A real paragraph beats fragments-as-style.
- **Numbered-list-as-synthesis** and **ladder of escalation** (stacking "and not only that…"). Use structure only when the content is genuinely list-shaped; Nullhype posts are paragraph-based anyway.

## Closers (Nullhype already bans CTAs — this is the full list)

- **CTA slop:** "What do you think? Drop your take below.", "Agree or disagree?", "What would you add?", "Follow for more X content.", "Repost if this resonated.", "Save this for later.", "Tag someone who needs to hear this.", "Link in comments."
- **Fake philosophical closer** (profound-sounding, says nothing): "The question isn't whether, but when.", "We're still early.", "The best X is yet to come."
- **Performative mic drop:** "Act accordingly.", "Welcome to the future.", "This changes everything.", "Buckle up." → Close on an earned, specific thesis instead.

## Word-level tells

- **Adverbs that scream AI** (3+ in a paragraph = AI wrote it): literally, incredibly, fundamentally, genuinely, essentially, significantly, arguably, undeniably, remarkably, interestingly, notably, particularly.
- **Adjectives that mean nothing:** robust, seamless, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, revolutionary, transformative, comprehensive, holistic, game-changing, next-level, world-class.
- **Verbs that sound smart but aren't:** leverage, navigate (challenges), unpack (concepts), double down, spearhead, supercharge, unlock, streamline, reimagine, synergize.

## Comment / reply slop (for high-signal replies)

- **Agreement slop / "this":** "This.", "Exactly this.", "Couldn't have said it better."
- **Engagement-farming comment:** generic questions that add nothing.
- **Humble-brag response:** "As someone who's been doing this for 10 years…", "I literally just had this conversation with my CEO.", "My team was just discussing this." → A reply must add a data point, correction, or precedent, or it doesn't ship.
- **Emoji tells:** strategic single emoji at the hook (🔥🚀💡🧵⚡🤯👀🎯📌) and per-bullet emojis. Nullhype uses no emojis in posts anyway.

## The principle (why this matters)

From the source: these phrases aren't bad because they're wrong, they're bad because they're *overused and invisible* — "when everything sounds the same, nothing sounds like anything." Nullhype's edge is sounding like a specific analyst with specific evidence, not like the median LinkedIn post.

## Self-check before publishing

1. Could the first two lines sit unchanged under any AI announcement? If yes, rewrite with a concrete actor/number/constraint.
2. Scan for any opener, pivot, fragmentation, or closer pattern above — cut or replace with a fact.
3. Count vague adverbs/adjectives/verbs; if a sentence leans on them instead of a number or mechanism, rewrite it.
4. Does every paragraph carry a fact, comparison, or advancement of the argument? If a line only decorates, delete it.
5. (House rule, not from the source) No em-dashes, no emojis, no generic CTA.
